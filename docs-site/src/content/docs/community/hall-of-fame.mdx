This project stands on years of work by data-platform maintainers, educators, and communities. This is a thank-you, not a claim that anyone listed here endorses Azure Data Lab Toolkit or that their project has already been integrated.

## Chrissy LeMaire and the dbatools community

[Chrissy LeMaire](https://dbatools.io/team/) created [dbatools](https://dbatools.io/), now maintained by its core team and contributors. Its practical PowerShell commands and migration workflows are important reference points for a PowerShell-first data lab.

## Brent Ozar and contributors

[Brent Ozar](https://www.brentozar.com/about/) and contributors maintain the [SQL Server First Responder Kit](https://github.com/BrentOzarULTD/SQL-Server-First-Responder-Kit). Brent also makes the [Stack Overflow database](https://www.brentozar.com/archive/2015/10/how-to-download-the-stack-overflow-database-via-bittorrent/) approachable for realistic SQL Server learning and performance work.

## Daniel Hutmacher

[Daniel Hutmacher](https://sqlsunday.com/about/) publishes prepared databases through [SQL Sunday](https://sqlsunday.com/downloads/) and keeps their underlying public-data sources visible. That care for provenance is a model for the planned catalog.

## WideWorldImporters authors

Microsoft's authoritative [WideWorldImporters README](https://github.com/microsoft/sql-server-samples/blob/master/samples/databases/wide-world-importers/README.md) credits [Greg Low](https://greglow.com/), [Denzil Ribeiro](https://www.linkedin.com/in/denzilribeiro), [Jos de Bruijn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/josdebruijn), and [Robert Cain](https://arcanecode.com/info/) as its authors. Their names should remain attached to the sample wherever it is used.

## Daniel Otykier

[Daniel Otykier](https://tabulareditor.com/what-is-tabular-editor) created [Tabular Editor](https://tabulareditor.com/), a foundational tool for tabular and semantic-model development. Its open-source and commercial editions also show why a catalog must distinguish product variants and terms.

## Mathias Thierbach

[Mathias Thierbach](https://github.com/mthierba) created and maintains [pbi-tools](https://pbi.tools/). His work is a strong example of source-controlled business-intelligence development.

## FUAM contributors

[Gellért Gintli](https://github.com/ggintli), [Kevin Thomas](https://github.com/kethom-analytics), and other contributors are associated with the development of [Fabric Unified Admin Monitoring](https://github.com/microsoft/fabric-toolbox/tree/main/monitoring/fabric-unified-admin-monitoring). The canonical repository and its history remain the authority for detailed contribution credit.

## Ola Hallengren

[Ola Hallengren](https://ola.hallengren.com/) created the [SQL Server Maintenance Solution](https://github.com/olahallengren/sql-server-maintenance-solution), showing how transparent operational scripts can become durable community infrastructure.

## Marco Russo and Alberto Ferrari

[Marco Russo](https://www.sqlbi.com/author/marco-russo/), [Alberto Ferrari](https://www.sqlbi.com/author/alberto-ferrari/), and the SQLBI team publish education, tools, and the [Contoso Data Generator](https://www.sqlbi.com/tools/contoso-data-generator/) for repeatable analytics examples.

## Gregor Brunner and the Measure Killer team

[Gregor Brunner and the Brunner BI team](https://measurekiller.com/about/) build [Measure Killer](https://measurekiller.com/) for semantic-model analysis and cleanup. It is a useful example of a commercial tool that may be demonstrated while remaining separately owned and licensed.

The list will grow carefully. Corrections and additions are welcome through [Suggest A Project](../suggest-a-project/); no social profile is listed unless it can be verified.
