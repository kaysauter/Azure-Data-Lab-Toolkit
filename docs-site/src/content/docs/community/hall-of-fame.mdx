This project stands on years of work by data-platform maintainers, educators, and communities. Inclusion here is gratitude and attribution, not a claim of endorsement or a shipped integration.

## Chrissy LeMaire and the dbatools community

[Chrissy LeMaire](https://dbatools.io/team/) created [dbatools](https://dbatools.io/), which grew into a community-maintained PowerShell toolkit for SQL Server. Its practical command design and migration workflows are important reference points for a PowerShell-first data lab.

## Brent Ozar and contributors

[Brent Ozar](https://www.brentozar.com/about/) and contributors maintain the [SQL Server First Responder Kit](https://github.com/BrentOzarULTD/SQL-Server-First-Responder-Kit). Brent also packages [Stack Overflow database downloads](https://www.brentozar.com/archive/2015/10/how-to-download-the-stack-overflow-database-via-bittorrent/) used in realistic SQL Server learning and performance work.

## Daniel Hutmacher

[Daniel Hutmacher](https://sqlsunday.com/about/) publishes reusable [SQL Sunday sample databases](https://sqlsunday.com/downloads/) and clearly credits their underlying public-data sources. That combination of useful demos and careful provenance is exactly what the planned catalog should preserve.

## Daniel Otykier

[Daniel Otykier](https://tabulareditor.com/what-is-tabular-editor) created [Tabular Editor](https://tabulareditor.com/), a foundational tool for tabular and semantic-model development. Its open-source and commercial editions also illustrate why catalog entries must distinguish product variants and licenses.

## Mathias Thierbach

[Mathias Thierbach](https://github.com/mthierba) created and maintains [pbi-tools](https://pbi.tools/) and contributes to the ecosystem around Microsoft's [Tabular Model Definition Language](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/analysis-services/tmdl/tmdl-overview). His work demonstrates what source-controlled business-intelligence development can look like.

## FUAM contributors

[Gellért Gintli](https://github.com/ggintli), [Kevin Thomas](https://github.com/kethom-analytics), and contributors developed the community accelerator now available as [Fabric Unified Admin Monitoring](https://github.com/microsoft/fabric-toolbox/tree/main/monitoring/fabric-unified-admin-monitoring) in Microsoft Fabric Toolbox.

## Ola Hallengren

[Ola Hallengren](https://ola.hallengren.com/) created the widely used [SQL Server Maintenance Solution](https://github.com/olahallengren/sql-server-maintenance-solution), showing how transparent, scriptable operational practices can become durable community infrastructure.

## Marco Russo and Alberto Ferrari

[Marco Russo](https://www.sqlbi.com/author/marco-russo/), [Alberto Ferrari](https://www.sqlbi.com/author/alberto-ferrari/), and SQLBI publish tools, education, and the [Contoso Data Generator](https://www.sqlbi.com/tools/contoso-data-generator/) for reproducible analytics examples.

## Gregor Brunner and the Measure Killer team

[Gregor Brunner and the Measure Killer team](https://measurekiller.com/about) build [Measure Killer](https://measurekiller.com/) for semantic-model analysis and cleanup. It is a useful example of a commercial community tool that may be demonstrated without being redistributed.

The list will grow carefully. Corrections and additions can be sent through [Suggest A Project](../suggest-a-project/).
